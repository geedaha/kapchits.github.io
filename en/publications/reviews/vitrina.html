Reviews

This page gathers the responses to Dr. Kapchits’s books and to his work. Where the text of a review was received from the author, it is published here in full and as received: the author obtained the reviewers’ consent to publication, and nothing in the texts has been changed. Copyright remains with the reviewers and their publications; each item names its source. Where only a bibliographic record exists, the item is given with its details and a summary. Until 6 September 2026 this page carried summaries in place of the texts; those summaries are kept in the project archive.

Work by colleagues

A. K. Zholkovsky. “The Somali Tale ‘A Soothsayer Tested’”

A condensed English version of the article published in Russian in the journal Narody Azii i Afriki, No. 1. Moscow, 1970. The English version appeared in: Essays in Honour of Muuse Ismaaciil Galaal. Ponte Invisibile, 2011, pp. 53–64.

This is not a review but a scholarly article by Dr. Kapchits’s teacher, devoted to the very tale that gave its title to the 2006 collection Faaliyihii la bilkeyday. Zholkovsky sets the tale apart from the rest of Somali folk prose for its philosophical content and finished form: the narration is interrupted three times by ‘acts’ in verse, which places it midway between poetry and the ordinary folktale. On pain of death a soothsayer must foretell the fate of his tribe; three times a serpent helps him, and three times he treats the serpent in a manner matching what has been predicted — in wartime he raises his sword, in the drought he withholds the promised share, in the year of plenty he brings everything he owns. In the closing monologue the serpent explains that this is not a matter of character: a person’s life echoes the structure of the world.

The analysis proceeds by the method of ‘generative poetics’: the theme is formulated as “revelation: life resembles the structure of the world” (A. Zholkovsky), and the article then shows step by step how the compositional decisions follow from it — the devices of simile and contrastive variation, the anti-theme of a formless, meaningless life, the splitting of the plot into a framing and a framed story, the motifs of contract, order and reward, and finally the sudden turn that makes the closing pronouncement not a moral tacked on to the tale but its denouement.

Tributes

A book about Kapchits (2022)

Abaal Raaga Rag Baa Leh by Cabdiraxmaan C. Faarax ‘Guri Barwaaqo’ (Hiil Press) — a collection of articles, interviews and appraisals of Kapchits’s contribution to Somali studies, presented to him by Somali colleagues. The author died on 3 March 2023; Kapchits’s obituary for him is in the project archive.

In March 2024 WardheerNews awarded Kapchits a certificate as Best Foreign Speaker — Expert in Somali Language (signed by Dr. Adan Makina, editor and head of the reviews and interviews section).

A dedication from Wolfgang Mieder

Wolfgang Mieder — professor at the University of Vermont and the field’s leading paremiologist — dedicated to Kapchits his annual International Bibliography of New and Reprinted Proverb Collections, which carries the line “For Georgi Kapchits” above the opening paragraph. The copy in the archive is the instalment the author labels as Proverbium 2018; the volume number is not printed on it.
